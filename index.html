What does AI actually do?

Artificial intelligence is software that learns patterns from examples and uses them to make predictions, decisions, and new content. Here's a plain-language tour.

What is AI?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science focused on building systems that can do tasks that normally need human intelligence: understanding language, recognizing images, solving problems, and making decisions.

Traditional programs follow rules a person writes step by step. Most modern AI is different: instead of being told the rules, it learns them by studying large amounts of examples. This approach is called machine learning.

How does AI learn?

  1. 1

    Collect data

    Gather lots of examples, like thousands of photos labeled "cat" or "dog", or millions of sentences of text.

  2. 2

    Train a model

    The computer adjusts millions of internal settings until its guesses match the examples as closely as possible.

  3. 3

    Make predictions

    The trained model handles new inputs it has never seen, such as a new photo or a new question.

  4. 4

    Improve

    People check the results, fix mistakes, and retrain with better data so the model keeps getting better.

What AI can't do (yet)

  • It doesn't truly understand. AI finds patterns; it doesn't have feelings, beliefs, or common sense the way people do.
  • It can be wrong with confidence. AI can produce answers that sound right but are false, so important results should be checked.
  • It learns our biases. If the training data is unfair or incomplete, the AI's decisions can be too.
  • It depends on people. Humans choose the data, set the goals, and are responsible for how AI is used.
